01 / POPULATION

2,000 customers

1,000 Retail and 1,000 Distributor customers, generating 16,000 customer-week observations.

02 / ASSIGNMENT

Persistent treatment group

Each customer is assigned once before the pre-period, preserving Treatment/Control identity across time.

03 / TIME

4 weeks pre + 4 post

Pre-period establishes baseline behaviour; post-period applies the pricing action to Treatment only.

04 / TREATMENT

5% price increase

Retail base price 100; Distributor 85. Control remains unchanged while Treatment increases 5%.

Demand assumptions

Segment-specific elasticity

  • Retail elasticity assumption: −1.8
  • Distributor elasticity assumption: −1.2
  • Observed simulated treatment elasticity: Retail −1.81; Distributor −1.26
  • Demand includes random variation around segment base demand
Economics

Contribution framework

  • Retail base demand: 50 units
  • Distributor base demand: 200 units
  • Cost modeled at 60% of price
  • Gross-margin dollars = (price − unit cost) × demand

Corrected methodology

What changed

The earlier draft assigned group only in the post period, which made treated and the DiD interaction collinear. The notebook has now been corrected so each customer is assigned before the pre-period and retains that identity across both periods.

  • Persistent Treatment/Control assignment
  • Valid pre/post group means
  • Welch t-tests for post-period comparisons
  • OLS Difference-in-Differences interaction
  • Elasticity diagnostics by segment
Decision governance

Evidence before rollout

  • Statistical significance is not the same as economic attractiveness
  • Review segment elasticity and contribution impact
  • Check customer retention, competitive response and contractual constraints
  • Use controlled rollout and holdout groups
  • Document decision, assumptions and post-action outcome
